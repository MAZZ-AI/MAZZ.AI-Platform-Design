# Notes from the kitchen and the call log

What we're learning rolling out AI call & chat handling across Cairo restaurants.

Pilot notes·Oct 28, 2026·6 min read

## Why Cairo restaurants are losing orders to unanswered phones

Friday at 8 PM is the best and worst hour of the week for a busy restaurant. The dining room is full, the kitchen is behind, and the phone is ringing. Someone has to decide, in real time, whether to drop a plate and answer it. Most nights, the phone loses.

That's not a staffing failure — it's math. A host running the floor can hold maybe two calls in a row before the line backs up or the call gets rushed. Every call after that either rings out or gets answered badly: half-heard orders, wrong delivery times, a customer who hangs up and orders from whoever picks up first.

"We weren't losing customers to bad food. We were losing them before they ever got a plate in front of them."

That's the problem MAZZ.AI was built around. **Every call gets answered on the first ring**, whether it's the quietest Tuesday lunch or the loudest Friday night. The AI knows the real menu, the real delivery zones, and the real wait time — because it's trained on your data, not a generic script.

It doesn't replace the floor staff. During the pilot, every restaurant starts at 25% AI and 75% human, with a person backing up every call. The ratio shifts month over month as the AI proves itself on your specific menu and your specific customers — never before.

The restaurants in our pilot aren't trying to automate hospitality. They're trying to stop losing orders to a phone nobody had a free hand to answer.
